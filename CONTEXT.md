# Scroll Court — Project Context

## Project Summary

Scroll Court is a Chrome extension and landing site for an anti-doomscrolling hackathon project.

The extension runs on YouTube Shorts and interrupts the user after they watch several Shorts. Instead of using guilt, timers, or lectures, Scroll Court uses comedy, quizzes, ancient philosopher parody quotes, and a roast-style receipt to make users realize how little they remember from mindless scrolling.

The main idea is that doomscrolling is not just a screen-time problem. It is an autopilot loop. Users keep consuming short-form content because there is no natural stopping point. Scroll Court creates that stopping point through humor.

## One-Line Pitch

Scroll Court is a Chrome extension where ancient philosophers judge your YouTube Shorts addiction, quiz your attention span, and issue a brutally funny receipt for your doomscrolling session.

## Problem

Short-form platforms like YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and Instagram Reels are designed with endless feeds and no natural stopping point.

Teens often doomscroll not because they truly want to, but because the content loop is frictionless:

- watch one video
- forget it
- swipe to the next
- repeat
- lose track of time

Traditional screen-time tools often rely on guilt, timers, blockers, or lectures. Teens usually ignore them because they feel annoying, preachy, or easy to bypass.

## Insight

The best way to interrupt doomscrolling is not shame. It is a funny, memorable pause.

Humor creates enough friction for the user to notice their autopilot behavior. The joke is the intervention.

Instead of saying:

> Stop scrolling. This is bad for you.

Scroll Court says:

> Socrates has summoned you to court because you watched 14 Shorts and retained absolutely nothing.

## Core Product Concept

Scroll Court interrupts YouTube Shorts sessions with a courtroom-style overlay.

The overlay includes:

1. A funny ancient philosopher parody quote
2. A quiz or attention check about the user’s scrolling
3. A wisdom rating system
4. Ranked titles based on behaviour
5. A final roast-style receipt at the end of the session
